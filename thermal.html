Where does the heat go?

A steady-state simulation of the base with every part in place, on a 1 mm grid. Heat comes from the Radxa's RK3566 (and the rest of the board), the SDR, the boost converter, the charger and the cell; it conducts through everything and leaves through the case's outside (natural convection + radiation). Pick a load and a cooling option, slice through the deck, or show everything above a temperature in 3D.

Estimates for comparing options, not lab measurements. Room 25 °C, lid open, deck on a desk. Sustained heavy load is shown un-throttled — the real SoC slows itself down around 85–95 °C.

What the simulation says

  • Vents barely help (−2 to −5 °C): the deck is only ~15 mm tall inside, so warm air hardly rises out.
  • An aluminium pillar from the SoC to an aluminium plate in the floor is the biggest win for the CPU (−15 °C typical, −39 °C heavy). There is ~15 mm of free air under the SoC for it.
  • A micro blower (15 mm, 0.1–0.2 L/s) cools the inside air, the battery and the SDR the most.
  • Free fixes: PETG/ASA instead of PLA (softens 20–40 °C later), keep the SDR off the hatch, power the SDR only when used, cap the CPU, don't charge during heavy use.
